# Anticipated Questions — Q&A Prep

Grouped by likely angle of attack. Each entry: **Q** (what they ask) → **A** (crisp response, ≤ 45 s aloud) → **Reserve** (where to go deeper if pressed).

---

## 1. Method — single-document primary corpus

**Q:** Your primary empirical material is one document. Isn't that a sample size of one? How do you defend the generalisability of your findings?

**A:** The unit of analysis is not "the EIC" or "EU innovation policy" — it is the **discursive construction of investment logics** inside a specific, high-stakes policy instrument. For that unit, the WP 2026 is a saturated case: 188 pages of dense normative-regulative text produced by a single institutional actor at a single moment. The three-pass coding surfaced 180 entries and stabilised on six patterns before the second pass — that stabilisation is the internal validity signal. Generalisability is **analytical, not statistical**: the patterns claim traction on other EU-level programmatic documents produced under similar institutional pressures, not on innovation policy in general.

**Reserve:** Point to Yin (case study logic), the mimetic benchmarks WP 2026 itself invokes (DARPA/Temasek/GGF) as evidence the document is aware of its own field position, and the coded cross-reference PDF as provenance.

---

**Q:** Why no interviews?

**A:** Two reasons. First, the RQ is about how logics are **constructed in text** — the object of analysis is the document's own performative work, not what its authors meant to do. Interviews would answer a different question. Second, resource and access constraints — Commission officials were not reachable within the project scope, and speculative interviews would have degraded rigour rather than added it. This is documented in Ch 3 §[TBD] and re-stated as a limitation in Ch 6.

**Reserve:** Suchman (1995) — legitimacy is analysed by studying the artefact through which it is claimed, not the intentions behind it.

## 2. Theory — why four bodies

**Q:** You use Scott, DiMaggio & Powell, Thornton, and Suchman. Isn't that theoretical over-reach for one thesis?

**A:** They do different jobs and do not substitute for each other. Scott gives the **pillars** — where in an institution a claim is anchored (regulative, normative, cognitive). DiMaggio & Powell give the **mechanism** — *how* claims travel between organisations (coercive, mimetic, normative). Thornton gives the **content** — *which* logic (Science, Market, State, Profession) a claim expresses. Suchman gives the **outcome** — how legitimacy is secured. Removing any one of them would leave the analysis unable to answer part of the RQ. The framework figure in Ch 2 shows how they compose.

**Reserve:** Point to specific findings that require the combination — e.g. Pattern F (TRL as cross-pillar legitimation artefact) is unstable without Scott's pillars, and Pattern B (Coercive Governance Cluster) is unstable without DiMaggio & Powell.

---

**Q:** Where is the Profession logic in your findings? You have Science, Market, State — but Profession seems absent.

**A:** Correct — Profession is present in the codebook but rarely dominant in WP 2026. The evaluators (peer experts) are the closest carriers, but the document subordinates them to the Programme Manager authority gradient (Pattern B). Its **relative absence** is itself a finding: expertise is instrumentalised inside a coercive-governance frame, not autonomised as a professional field.

**Reserve:** Codebook v2 §[X] — Profession codings by count; contrast with Science (dominant at low TRL) and State (dominant at high TRL).

## 3. Findings — the six patterns

**Q:** Pattern E claims the state constructs the market signal it then requires. Isn't that just how blended finance works?

**A:** That's the point — but WP 2026 doesn't present STEP as blended finance. It presents it as market catalysis. The **circularity** is what the pattern names: state capital is deployed to generate co-investment which the state then treats as evidence of market interest which justifies further state capital. The pattern makes visible a rhetorical move that the document works to keep invisible. That's the institutional-theory contribution.

**Reserve:** Mertens & Thiemann, Gabor — critical blended-finance scholarship (see `01_literature/output/critical_blended_finance_to_consult.md`). Pattern E is analytically adjacent to their critiques but arrived at through document-internal coding, not applied from outside.

---

**Q:** Pattern A is "tension-dissolution" — competing logics presented as complementary. How do you know this is a **rhetorical** move and not just a genuine complementarity?

**A:** Two markers. First, the coding rule (rule 1, `LOGIC_TENSION` surface-visibility) requires that the tension be **traceable in the text itself** — otherwise the code is not applied. Second, the pattern is confirmed by contrast: in five of six patterns the document performs some kind of harmonisation work; the sixth (Pattern D, Challenge chapter succession) is a pure analytical observation without harmonisation. The consistency of the harmonisation move across A, B, C, E, F is what makes it a pattern rather than a coincidence.

**Reserve:** Pattern-by-pattern excerpt evidence — the coded PDF.

---

**Q:** You tested a Sustainability template and did not confirm it. Why include a null result in a thesis?

**A:** Because analytical honesty requires it, and because the four preserved entry mechanisms serve as **analytical contrast** with Pattern C (State Social Equity Template). Without the null, the reader has no way to see why Pattern C stabilised as a template while Sustainability did not — that contrast is itself informative about which normative claims WP 2026 institutionalises and which it merely mentions.

## 4. Methodology — GenAI and reflexivity

**Q:** You disclose GenAI-assisted analysis. How do we know the patterns aren't artefacts of the model?

**A:** Three safeguards. First, the deductive Pass 1 was coded against fixed a-priori codebooks derived from Scott / DiMaggio-Powell / Thornton — the codes were not generated by the model. Second, all 180 entries carry explicit page references to WP 2026, so every coding is provenance-verifiable. Third, the manual colour-coded physical copy (see `05_analysis/output/colour-coded-eic-doc.pdf`) was produced independently and cross-referenced against the digital coding — where they diverged is documented. The model assisted the work; it did not author the findings.

**Reserve:** Methodology-GenAI references in `01_literature/references/methodology_genai/`; the seven coding rules in Codebook v2 §9 as adjudication surface.

## 5. Contribution — so what

**Q:** What does this thesis contribute that a policy analyst couldn't have written?

**A:** A policy analyst would tell you what STEP does. This thesis tells you **how STEP works as a legitimation artefact** — what institutional pressures it responds to, which logics it composes, and where the composition papers over tensions. That's the institutional-theory move: not evaluation of the instrument but analysis of its performative structure. It matters because instruments that dissolve tensions rhetorically often reproduce them operationally, and knowing which tensions have been dissolved is a diagnostic for where the instrument may under-deliver.

## 6. Limitations you should raise before they do

- **Single-author interpretive analysis** — no inter-coder reliability check. Mitigated by explicit coding rules and provenance, but limits the epistemic weight of the pattern claims.
- **Point-in-time document** — WP 2026 will be superseded; the findings speak to *this* WP, not to the EIC in perpetuity.
- **No engagement with implementation data** — the analysis is about the text, not about what happens when the text is executed. That is a next project, not this one.

## 7. Reserve — questions worth rehearsing but unlikely

- "Why not use Bourdieu / field theory instead of Scott?" — because the RQ is about how logics are *composed in text*, not about actors' positions in a field. Bourdieu would answer a different question.
- "Why British English?" — DTU convention; consistency, not a claim.
- "Are you critical of the EIC?" — the analysis is analytical, not evaluative. Where the patterns imply tensions, I name them. Whether that is "critical" depends on the reader's priors.
