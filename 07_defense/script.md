# Speaker Script — opener, transitions, closer

Not a verbatim script for every slide. Just the beats that must land word-perfectly: opener, RQ statement, hardest transitions, closer.

**Audience assumption:** both Norn and the external censor have read the thesis. Script register is *analytical performance*, not explanation. Beat numbering follows `outline.md` v2 (7 beats).

---

## Opener (Beat 1)

~30 s. Straight in — no throat-clearing. Voice: `../06_report/voice_card.md`.

> Draghi called for scale, Letta called for depth, and in the middle of that argument the European Commission has moved to integrate STEP directly into the European Innovation Council. Work Programme 2026 is the first document to carry that integration at instrument level. This thesis takes the text as its object — not to evaluate what the instrument does, but to analyse how it composes the investment logics it operates through.

Delivery notes:
- Pace: slow on "composes the investment logics" — that's the analytical move landing.
- Bridge into RQ verbatim: "Which brings me to the research question."

## RQ statement (Beat 1, verbatim)

> How are investment logics constructed, legitimised and contested within the EIC instruments Pathfinder and STEP, and how can these dynamics be understood through the lens of institutional theory?

## Setting → Framework transition (Beat 2 → Beat 3)

~5 s.

> Which is why the analysis proceeds through four bodies of theory rather than one.

## Mimetic-correction beat (Beat 3, centrepiece)

~1:15 min. This is the honest moment — do not perform it, deliver it. First person is authorised here (reflexivity register).

> The methodology chapter discloses GenAI assistance. The most honest test of the safeguards is a moment where I had to correct my own reading.
>
> Early in the coding I concluded that the corpus explicitly benchmarks against DARPA, Temasek, and the Chinese Government Guidance Funds. That reading held for several drafts. When I went back to check the text at Strategic Goal 4, where the mimicry claim was anchored, the corpus says "USA and Asia". No named comparator. The explicit-benchmark claim was mine, not the document's.
>
> I traced the error back to the literature I had been reading in parallel — Mazzucato, Karo and Kattel, Bonvillian — which does name those comparators. That literature had leaked into how I read the primary text.
>
> The reading was not wrong. DiMaggio and Powell's original definition of mimetic isomorphism is structural resemblance, not textual acknowledgement. The finding survived: the Work Programme mirrors the developmental-state template. The framing changed. I corrected it across chapters, and the audit trail sits in synthesis section 0.5.
>
> What matters here is that the safeguard worked. The provenance check, where every code cites a page, caught the drift. The methodology stance is operational, not decorative.

Delivery notes:
- Slow the second paragraph. Let "The explicit-benchmark claim was mine, not the document's" sit for a beat.
- Do not smile through this. It is honest, not embarrassing.
- If a panel member interrupts to probe here, that is the pre-emptive-credit paying off — take the question.

## Framework/Method → Findings transition (Beat 3 → Beat 4)

## Headline finding — the one line that must land

> Across the TRL axis, the Work Programme organises itself as a logic succession — Science, then Market, then State — and this succession is reproduced in miniature inside each Challenge chapter.

## Findings → Discussion transition (Beat 4 → Beat 5)

~10 s.

> The Seal is where the findings stop and the interpretation begins. Three moves in the Discussion — a structural comparison, an open question the corpus invites, and what any of this means for practice.

## Closer (Beat 7)

~1 min total. Two lines on slide (from `outline.md` Beat 7, verbatim). Narration below is what wraps them.

> To answer the research question directly.
>
> [read line 1 from slide] Investment logics in the EIC's Pathfinder and STEP are composed through a Science–Market–State succession that dissolves tensions at multiple scales — passage, chapter, instrument.
>
> The succession is the headline. The dissolution is the mechanism. Six patterns and seven coding rules stabilised on that reading.
>
> [pause; second line comes up on slide]
>
> And the open question. [read line 2 from slide] Whether the Sovereignty Seal is a first move toward demand-side statecraft under state-aid constraints — a question the corpus invites but does not itself answer.
>
> The Seal is coded across three functions. Whether it becomes a portable credential inside Member State procurement channels is a question the next Work Programme, or the next thesis, will settle.
>
> Thank you. I am happy to take questions.

Delivery notes:
- Do not rush the pause between the two slide lines. That silence is the closer's rhetorical move — it hands the panel the open question deliberately, not accidentally.
- Land "the corpus invites but does not itself answer" as the final analytical line. Everything after is stage business.
- Stand still through "Thank you." Do not reach for water.
