# Speaker Script — beat-by-beat

Delivery-ready prose for every beat, plus delivery notes.

**Word-perfect** (rehearse until they land clean): opener, RQ verbatim, mimetic-correction, headline finding, closer.

**Delivery-ready** (speak from the paragraphs, not verbatim — the audience should feel you have thought this through, not that you are reading): everything else.

**Audience assumption:** both Norn and the external censor have read the thesis. Script register is *analytical performance*, not explanation. Beat numbering follows `outline.md` v2 (7 beats).

Voice card: `../06_report/voice_card.md`. British English throughout.

## Beat 1 — Opener (~30 s)

Straight in — no throat-clearing.

> Draghi called for scale, Letta called for depth, and in the middle of that argument the European Commission has moved to integrate STEP directly into the European Innovation Council. Work Programme 2026 is the first document to carry that integration at instrument level. This thesis takes the text as its object — not to evaluate what the instrument does, but to analyse how it composes the investment logics it operates through.

Delivery notes:
- Pace: slow on "composes the investment logics" — that's the analytical move landing.
- Bridge into RQ verbatim: "Which brings me to the research question."

## Beat 2 — Empirical setting + corpus (~1:30)

Compressed. Both readers know what the EIC is. Foreground what makes *this slice* interesting.

> Work Programme 2026 is the first document to carry STEP at instrument level inside the EIC. That is why it is the corpus — it captures a hybrid instrument at first instance, and it does so at the moment the state's role in EU industrial policy is under active contest.
>
> The primary corpus is the Pathfinder and STEP Scale-Up sections, sliced to roughly 110 pages. The Horizon Europe Regulation and STEP Regulation 2024/795 are cited legal instruments, not primary corpus.
>
> Scope: the analysis is on the discursive construction of investment logics inside these two instruments. Not on implementation, not on other EIC instruments, not on EU innovation policy in general. That constraint is a deliberate choice with the supervisor; the alternative was a shallower comparative study, and depth on one instrument earns more analytical traction than breadth across several.

Delivery notes:
- If a panel member probes the pivot from a comparative RQ to a single-document RQ, take the question here — do not defer. The honest supervisor-scoping account in `topics.md` is the response.
- Do not dwell on scale (110 pages). It is context, not defence.

## Beat 3 — Framework composition (~45 s)

One slide with the composition figure. Skip per-theory recaps — reserve slide has them.

> Four bodies of theory compose one analytical spine. Scott gives the pillars — where in an institution a claim is anchored, regulative, normative, or cognitive. DiMaggio and Powell give the mechanism — how claims travel between organisations, coercive, mimetic, normative. Thornton gives the content — which logic a claim expresses, Science, Market, State, Profession. Suchman gives the outcome — how legitimacy is secured, pragmatic, moral, cognitive.
>
> Removing any one of them breaks a specific finding. The framework figure in Chapter 2 shows the composition. The pattern-by-pattern loading sits in synthesis section 2.

Delivery notes:
- The four-lines list is dense; slow one word per line: *pillars, mechanism, content, outcome*.
- Say "removing any one of them breaks a specific finding" as a full sentence, not a throwaway. This is the pre-emptive answer to the theoretical-over-reach probe.

## Beat 3 — Method pipeline + single-coder limitation (~1 min)

One slide with the three-pass pipeline diagram.

> Documentary analysis. The unit of analysis is the discursive construction inside the text, not authorial intent. Interviews would answer a different question.
>
> Three-pass pipeline. Pass 1 is deductive, 180 entries coded against a-priori codebooks drawn from Scott and DiMaggio-Powell. Pass 2 is inductive, 29 flags resolved into six patterns and seven coding rules. Pass 3 is the writing. GenAI-assisted throughout, with three safeguards named in Chapter 3: the a-priori codebooks fix the initial code set, page provenance ties every entry to a specific line in the corpus, and a manual colour-coded physical copy of the document was cross-referenced against the digital coding as an independent check.
>
> One coder. That is a limitation. It is mitigated by the codified rules and the provenance, not eliminated.

Delivery notes:
- Do not read the safeguards as a list. Deliver them as three sentences with distinct emphasis.
- Land "not eliminated" flatly. Owning the limitation cleanly here means the panel does not need to extract it from you in Q&A.

## Beat 3 — Mimetic-correction centrepiece (~1:15)

This is the honest moment — do not perform it, deliver it. First person is authorised here (reflexivity register).

> The methodology chapter discloses GenAI assistance. The most honest test of the safeguards is a moment where I had to correct my own reading.
>
> Early in the coding I concluded that the corpus explicitly benchmarks against DARPA, Temasek, and the Chinese Government Guidance Funds. That reading held for several drafts. When I went back to check the text at Strategic Goal 4, where the mimicry claim was anchored, the corpus says "USA and Asia". No named comparator. The explicit-benchmark claim was mine, not the document's.
>
> I traced the error back to the literature I had been reading in parallel — Mazzucato, Karo and Kattel, Bonvillian — which does name those comparators. That literature had leaked into how I read the primary text.
>
> The reading was not wrong. DiMaggio and Powell's original definition of mimetic isomorphism is structural resemblance, not textual acknowledgement. The finding survived: the Work Programme mirrors the developmental-state template. The framing changed. I corrected it across chapters, and the audit trail sits in synthesis section 0.5.
>
> What matters here is that the safeguard worked. The provenance check, where every code cites a page, caught the drift. The methodology stance is operational, not decorative.

## Beat 4.1 — Headline finding + Pattern D (~2 min)

Slide: TRL axis with Science → Market → State labels.

> Across the TRL axis, the Work Programme organises itself as a logic succession — Science, then Market, then State — and this succession is reproduced in miniature inside each Challenge chapter.
>
> That is the headline, and it operates at two scales at once. In aggregate across the TRL axis the succession is what you would expect: Science at Pathfinder, Market in transition, State at Scale-Up. What you would not expect, and what Pattern D formalises, is that the same succession is reproduced inside each thematic Challenge chapter. Not just across the instrument. Structural inside every block.
>
> This is the kind of pattern you only see once you have coded provenance. Each Challenge chapter reads like a self-contained micro-instrument that walks through the full logic sequence. That structural repetition is what makes the succession a template rather than an artefact of aggregation.

Delivery notes:
- "The kind of pattern you only see once you have coded provenance" — earn this line. It is a soft defence of the method.
- Do not linger. Beat 4 is a crescendo; the headline is the base, not the peak.

## Beat 4.2 — Pattern A + state-logic surprise (~2:30)

Slide: Pattern A one-liner + the rule 7 register-vs-organising split.

> Pattern A — Tension-Dissolution. Competing logics presented as complementary. Rule 1 keeps the code honest: the tension has to be traceable in the text before I apply the label, otherwise the reading is mine and not the document's.
>
> Where Pattern A got structurally interesting was at instrument level. The EIC narrative uses market vocabulary — Venture Debt, equity, deal flow, co-investment — and it competes directly with market actors on that vocabulary. Rule 7 codifies the check: register versus organising. Does the document sound market-y, or does it actually organise market mechanisms? On the register side the market vocabulary is dense. On the organising side the underlying logic codes as state. Programme Manager authority, Commission conditionalities, sovereign access architecture.
>
> This is the finding that surprised me. The instrument narrates itself as bridging academia to private capital. The coding says the underlying logic is dominated by state, with market vocabulary doing legitimation work. Pattern A dissolves that tension by presenting the two as complementary rather than in conflict.
>
> The probe I want to pre-empt: isn't this just state aid dressed up as market? No. State aid is a legal category under Article 107. Pattern A at instrument level is an institutional-legitimation move, not a state-aid workaround. Those are different registers, and the analysis distinguishes them.

Delivery notes:
- Slow on "state, with market vocabulary doing legitimation work." That is the analytical claim.
- The state-aid probe pre-empt is a gift — deliver it as though you would ask it yourself.

## Beat 4.3 — Pattern E — STEP Circular Market Validation (~2 min)

Slide: the circular diagram (state capital → co-investment → market signal → state capital).

> Pattern E — STEP Circular Market Validation. State capital deploys, generates co-investment, the co-investment gets treated as a market signal, and the market signal justifies further state capital. The circle closes on itself.
>
> Rule 2, the STEP catalytic co-coding, is what codifies the read at coding time — every STEP reference to catalytic capital gets checked against whether the market signal it invokes has been constructed by state action upstream.
>
> This is analytically adjacent to Gabor's critique of the Wall Street Consensus, and to Mertens and Thiemann on de-risking. But Pattern E was arrived at through document-internal coding, not applied from outside. That matters because it means the pattern is grounded in what the Work Programme itself does, not in what a critical literature says instruments like STEP tend to do.
>
> What the pattern makes visible is a rhetorical move the document works to keep invisible. The document says market catalysis. The coding says the state constructs the signal it then requires.

Delivery notes:
- Land "the circle closes on itself" as the pattern's tagline. Everything after unpacks it.
- Distinguish the pattern from the critical literature explicitly. Not applying Gabor from outside; converging with Gabor from inside.

## Beat 4.4 — Sovereignty Seal (~2 min)

Slide: the three coded functions with entry numbers.

> The Sovereignty Seal is coded across three functions in the text. It unlocks InvestEU Venture Debt at entries 152 and 161. It unlocks the Business Acceleration Services. And, at entry 166, it is awardable on a No-Go decision — the applicant does not get STEP funding, but they still leave with the Seal.
>
> That third function is the analytical door. The Seal decouples from the investment decision. The state certifies strategic value independently of committing capital. That is a distinctive move, and it is the one the Findings surface but do not interpret — interpretation is the Discussion's job.
>
> The Seal is field-bridging. The same credential travels across public EU programmes and private investors. Synthesis section 2.8 develops it as a portable legitimacy artefact.
>
> I am understating the Seal here deliberately. Findings gives you the artefact. Discussion asks what it is doing.

Delivery notes:
- "Decouples from the investment decision" is the coded finding. Land it precisely — it is what the Discussion later builds on.
- Do not preview the Discussion move here. The rhetorical structure of the talk depends on the Seal opening the interpretive space at Beat 5, not at Beat 4.

## Beat 5.1 — STEP as incomplete developmental-state mimicry (~2 min)

Slide: three-column comparative table — DARPA, Chinese GGF, STEP — with supply and demand rows.

> DARPA runs on supply plus demand. Missions are constructed from Department of Defense demand — solve a DARPA mission and you know demand exists from the largest single customer on the planet.
>
> Chinese Government Guidance Funds run on supply plus demand. Capital plus state-owned enterprise off-take. The entrepreneur has both channels integrated inside the same statecraft.
>
> STEP runs on supply only. State-backed capital deployed through a hybrid instrument. No integrated demand-side channel inside the instrument.
>
> That is not an oversight. That is a structural feature of doing developmental-state work under Article 107 state-aid constraints. Demand-side statecraft is the under-designed side of the Work Programme architecture, and the constraint that under-designs it is legal, not analytical.
>
> The comparison is structural — DiMaggio and Powell's original meaning of mimetic isomorphism. Read against Mazzucato on the entrepreneurial state, Karo and Kattel on state capacity, Bonvillian on DARPA. And read against the corpus's own generic "USA and Asia" reference, per the correction I named earlier.

Delivery notes:
- The three-line supply/demand comparison must land clean. Rehearse until it does.
- "The constraint that under-designs it is legal, not analytical" is the interpretive move earning the LO on external context.

## Beat 5.2 — The Sovereignty Seal as candidate demand-side instrument (~2 min)

Slide: Seal → BAS → Chapter VII → VII.5 chain, with a labelled marker for "outside primary corpus".

> This is the open question the work leaves me with. Not a finding — a reflection register.
>
> The Sovereignty Seal chain runs from the Seal itself to Business Acceleration Services to Chapter VII of the Work Programme. Chapter VII contains section VII.5 — Strategic Access to Innovation Procurement. That body is outside the sliced primary corpus, which is why this is positioned as reflection rather than finding.
>
> The Seal is ambiguous by design. It does not say "for procurement". It says "strategically important". That ambiguity is what lets it work across two channels at once — investor legitimacy on one side, procurement discretion on the other. A Commission-issued credential that Member State procurement authorities could pick up under existing procurement law, without triggering Article 107 state-aid review.
>
> Whether that is the intent, I do not know. The corpus invites the reading; it does not confirm it. Adjacencies worth naming — Mazzucato on mission-oriented policy, Karo and Kattel on state capacity, Draghi and Letta on the demand-side gap in EU industrial strategy. I am naming these, not overclaiming engagement with them.

Delivery notes:
- Say "Not a finding — a reflection register" as a full sentence with a stop. That signposts the epistemic move honestly.
- The "outside the sliced primary corpus" caveat is not throwaway. Deliver it as the honest bounding of the interpretive move.

## Beat 5.3 — Practice implications (~1 min)

Slide: three practice reads, one line each.

> Three practice reads. All of them consolidate what the analysis implies for people who work with instruments like this, not what the analysis proves.
>
> For a policy officer designing the next instrument: the Work Programme 2026 architecture shows demand-side statecraft as the under-designed side. The Seal is a candidate design pattern for future instruments — a decoupled credential that carries strategic weight without carrying capital.
>
> For an EIC evaluator: the Seal is not a consolation prize. It is an operational credential. Treat it as separable from the investment decision — which is how the Work Programme itself already positions it at entry 166.
>
> For a would-be STEP applicant: the Seal has downstream value — InvestEU access, Business Acceleration Services gateway, procurement discretion signal — independent of whether STEP funds the round.

Delivery notes:
- "Consolidate what the analysis implies, not what it proves" is the epistemic honesty framing.
- Do not linger on any one of the three. If a panel member wants to press one, they will pick it up in Q&A.

## Beat 6 — Limitations (~1 min)

Slide: four bullets, no elaboration on-slide.

> Four limitations, briefly.
>
> Single-document primary corpus. The unit of analysis is discursive construction inside this instrument, not EU innovation policy in general.
>
> Single-coder interpretive analysis. Mitigated by codified rules, page provenance, manual cross-reference. Not eliminated.
>
> Point in time. Work Programme 2026 will be superseded. The patterns speak to how the document does its institutional work — transferable in mechanism if not in specifics.
>
> No implementation data. Studied the text, not the deployment. That is the natural next project, not this one.

Delivery notes:
- Fast, flat delivery. Owning limitations cleanly is the register; do not dramatise them.
