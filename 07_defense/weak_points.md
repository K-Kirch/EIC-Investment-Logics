# Weak Points — self-audit

Honest list of where the panel will probe hardest. For each: the vulnerability, the pre-cooked response, and whether to raise it *before* the panel does.

**Audience assumption:** both Norn and the external censor have read the thesis. Probes will land as sharpening (Norn) or as trained-eye external reads (censor), not as ignorant attacks. Beat numbering follows `outline.md` v2 (7 beats).

## 1. Sample size = 1 primary document

**Vulnerability:** Trivially easy attack line — "one document isn't research".
**Response:** Unit of analysis is the discursive construction inside the instrument (not "EIC policy" in general). WP 2026 is a saturated case for that unit. See `anticipated_questions.md` §1.
**Raise proactively?** Yes — in Beat 3 (framework + method) and Beat 6 (limitations), name the constraint before they name it.

## 2. Interpretive coding by a single coder

**Vulnerability:** No inter-coder reliability check.
**Response:** Mitigated by (a) explicit a-priori deductive codes in Pass 1, (b) provenance — every entry cites a page, (c) seven codified adjudication rules in Codebook v2 §9, (d) manual colour-coded cross-reference PDF as independent second pass.
**Raise proactively?** Yes — briefly in Beat 3 (single-coder limitation line, ~15 sec) and again in Beat 6 (limitations). Better to own it than to have it exposed.

## 3. GenAI in the analytical pipeline

**Vulnerability:** "How do we know the model didn't invent the patterns?"
**Response:** See `anticipated_questions.md` §4. Three-safeguard answer.
**Raise proactively?** Yes — the methodology chapter discloses it; the defence should re-state disclosure in Beat 3 (three safeguards + mimetic-correction anecdote as live evidence the safeguards worked).

## 4. Framework stacking

**Vulnerability:** Four bodies of theory in one MSc thesis — could look like theoretical over-reach.
**Response:** Each does a distinct job (pillars / mechanism / content / outcome). Removing any one breaks a specific finding. See `anticipated_questions.md` §2.
**Raise proactively?** No — but be ready to name the composition move on the framework slide (Beat 3).

## 5. Sustainability template not confirmed

**Vulnerability:** Looks like a failure — "you tried and it didn't work".
**Response:** Analytical honesty; four preserved entry mechanisms serve as contrast with Pattern C. See `anticipated_questions.md` §3 last question.
**Raise proactively?** Yes — briefly in Beat 4.5 (findings brief mentions). Naming a null result signals rigour.

## 6. Critical blended-finance literature (Gabor, Mertens & Thiemann, Wigger, Karo & Kattel)

**Vulnerability:** These voices are named in the "to consult" list but may not be fully engaged in the final Ch 2. If the panel has read them, they'll probe.
**Response:** Pattern E is analytically adjacent to their critiques but was arrived at through document-internal coding, not applied top-down. Name the adjacency; do not overclaim engagement.
**Raise proactively?** No — but have the Mertens & Thiemann name ready if it comes up.

## 7. Point-in-time analysis

**Vulnerability:** WP 2026 will be superseded — what does the thesis contribute after that?
**Response:** The patterns are about *how* an EU-level programmatic document does its institutional work. That mechanism is transferable even if the specific WP is not. See `anticipated_questions.md` §5 (contribution).
**Raise proactively?** Yes — in Beat 6 (limitations).

## 8. No engagement with implementation data

**Vulnerability:** "You've studied the text. What about what actually happens?"
**Response:** Different project. Own it as a limitation and as a natural next step.
**Raise proactively?** Yes — Beat 6.
