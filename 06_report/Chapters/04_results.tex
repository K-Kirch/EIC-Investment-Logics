\chapter{Findings}
\label{ch:results}

\section*{Chapter overview}

This chapter presents the empirical findings of the readthrough of the
Pathfinder and STEP Scale Up sections of the EIC Work Programme 2026,
following the order in which the synthesis builds them. The chapter
opens with a logic profile across the corpus, then sets out the six
patterns confirmed in Pass~2 (Patterns A--F), then collects the
STEP-specific findings that do not themselves form patterns, and
closes with the sustainability-entry inventory, the candidate template
that the analysis did not confirm. The register throughout is
descriptive: the analytical interpretation of these findings belongs
in Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}.

\section{Logic profile by section}
\label{sec:res-profile}

The corpus distributes its institutional logics unevenly across the
two instruments. The Pathfinder side opens in
\texttt{LOGIC\_SCIENCE} at the level of objectives, surrounds it with
\texttt{LOGIC\_STATE} in scope and impact framings, and routes its
governance through \texttt{LOGIC\_PROFESSION}. The STEP side is
dominated by \texttt{LOGIC\_STATE}, with \texttt{LOGIC\_MARKET}
genuinely operative only in the award criteria of Table~9 and a small
set of investment-mechanics passages. The profile in
\cref{tab:res-profile} reproduces \texttt{synthesis\_v2.md~\S1}; the
rest of the chapter unpacks the patterns that produce it.

\paragraph{Logic succession across the innovation pipeline.}
Reading the profile from top to bottom is reading the EIC's
innovation pipeline from low TRL to high. As the technology matures
from proof-of-principle to scale-up, the dominant institutional logic
shifts. \texttt{LOGIC\_SCIENCE} organises the Pathfinder Open
objectives at TRL~1--4. \texttt{LOGIC\_STATE} progressively organises
the impact framings of the Pathfinder Challenges.
\texttt{LOGIC\_STATE} dominates the STEP architecture at
commercialisation stage, with \texttt{LOGIC\_MARKET} present mainly
as a constrained evaluative pole in Table~9. The succession is not a
smooth transition. It is punctuated by embedded market requirements
in science-phase Challenges ([102], [109]) and by science criteria in
the STEP award structure ([169]). These cross-logic embeddings are
where the patterns of the rest of the chapter live.

\paragraph{Intra-chapter reproduction.}
A second, finer-grained reproduction of the same succession is
observable inside each Challenge chapter (see
\cref{sec:res-pattern-d}, Pattern~D). Scope and background frame the
Challenge in \texttt{LOGIC\_STATE} terms; the Specific Objectives
shift to \texttt{LOGIC\_SCIENCE}; the Portfolio Approach routes
through \texttt{LOGIC\_PROFESSION}; the Expected Impact returns to
\texttt{LOGIC\_STATE}. The macro-level succession across the pipeline
is therefore reproduced at chapter scale across substantively
different technology domains. The meso-level reading of this
reproduction is taken up in \Cref{ch:discussion}.

\section{Pattern A --- Tension-Dissolution}
\label{sec:res-pattern-a}

Pattern~A names a recurring textual move: where two institutional
logics could be presented as in conflict, the text instead presents
them as naturally complementary, joining them with \enquote{and} or
listing them as parallel sub-criteria. The tension is structurally
present, but the text does not acknowledge it. The conflicts the
analysis is most interested in (open science against
intellectual-property exploitation, scientific curiosity against
market readiness, \enquote{any field} against \enquote{named
technology domains}) are exactly the ones the text passes over in
silence.

\paragraph{Distinction from \texttt{LOGIC\_TENSION}.}
The \texttt{LOGIC\_TENSION} code applies only where the conflict is
legible in the text itself, through hedging, explicit acknowledgement
of competing priorities, or structural segmentation that visibly
contains the tension. Pass~2 introduced this as coding rule~1 (the
surface-visibility standard for \texttt{LOGIC\_TENSION}), and
Pattern~A is its analytical counterpart: it names the cases where
conflict is structurally present but rhetorically suppressed. Several
Pass~1 codings of \texttt{LOGIC\_TENSION} were withdrawn in Pass~2
once rule~1 was formalised, including [003], [008], and [019]. In
each case the underlying configuration is a Pattern~A signature
rather than a surface-visible tension.

\paragraph{Instances.}
The pattern is observable across the corpus, but it is most
concentrated in three locations.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Overview and Strategic Goals.} The Strategic Goals
    paragraph at [003] presents science, market, and state logics as
    co-implicated in a single \enquote{research base across the EU},
    quietly reframing what counts as valuable science. The Overview
    paragraph at [008] arranges \enquote{any field}
    (\texttt{LOGIC\_SCIENCE}) and \enquote{named technology domains}
    (\texttt{LOGIC\_STATE}) as a continuous arc of instrument design
    rather than as alternatives.
  \item \textbf{Pathfinder Open evaluation criteria.} The Excellence
    criterion at [056] folds market vocabulary
    (\enquote{high-risk/high-gain}, \enquote{radically new
    technology}) into a science-quality criterion without
    acknowledging that the two pull in different directions. The
    Impact criterion at [057] lists market success and societal or
    environmental impact as equivalent sub-criteria. The gatekeepers
    passage at [044] presents three governance criteria (what the
    instrument produces, how it works, and who it admits) as
    complementary rather than as drawn from different logics.
  \item \textbf{Challenge Specific Objectives.} [102] self-identifies
    as a Pattern~A instance: \enquote{potential markets and
    associated impacts} are appended to science objectives via
    \enquote{also}, subordinate but mandatory. [109] performs the
    same move at the Healthy Ageing Specific Objectives, listing
    science proof-of-concept and market-exploitation deliverables as
    co-equal requirements. The tension surfaces only when [108],
    which acknowledges the low translational success rate in ageing
    research, is read against [109].
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The most consequential instance.}
[082] embeds state-defined Challenge relevance as the primary
sub-criterion of scientific excellence. Applicants must frame their
science to fit a state-defined Challenge in order to receive funding,
and the text presents this as a quality-of-science criterion rather
than as a state-organising one. This is Pattern~A operating at the
gate of access: it shapes how science is allowed to be science in the
Challenge calls.

\paragraph{Pattern~A as a textual strategy.}
The instances above share a structural feature: logics that conflict
in practice are presented as if they did not. The strategy performs
institutional coherence rather than arguing for it; it is silent
about exactly the conflicts the readthrough surfaces. Whether this
silence is best read as a cognitive-legitimacy strategy presenting
the instrument as obviously coherent, as a managed compromise between
mandates that cannot otherwise be reconciled in a single text, or as
something else, is the interpretive question taken up in
\Cref{ch:discussion}.

\section{Pattern B --- Coercive governance cluster}
\label{sec:res-pattern-b}

Pattern~B names a cluster of passages in which the EIC Programme
Manager holds discretionary authority over capital continuity,
roadmap direction, portfolio placement, and inter-consortium
collaboration. The cluster begins with a stated protection of
applicant autonomy and ends in soft collaboration directives, but the
intermediate passages place decisive control in the hands of a hired
official acting under the grant agreement. Read as a configuration,
the cluster forms an intensity gradient that runs from hard
contractual sanction to soft directive language.

\paragraph{The intensity gradient.}
The gradient is observable across seven entries. The stated autonomy
protection sits at [021]: applicants choose their research direction
within the scope of their proposal. [023] introduces milestone
oversight by the Programme Manager. [024] grants the Agency authority
to suspend or terminate a grant where the project no longer addresses
the Challenge it was funded under. [025] directs portfolio placement
in softer language: projects \enquote{may be requested to join} a
thematic portfolio. [026] and [027] formalise proactive
roadmap-setting and steering by the Programme Manager. [061] sets a
dual-responsiveness mandate without resolving how competing
directions are to be reconciled. [115] closes the gradient at the
soft end: consortia are \enquote{encouraged to collaborate} across
the portfolio. The gradient is a property of the cluster as a whole,
not of any single passage, and is recorded here as Pattern~B
(observation) rather than promoted to a code.

\paragraph{Rule~3 and the frozen-notes consequence.}
Pass~2 introduced coding rule~3, which extends \texttt{ISO\_COERCIVE}
to contractual discretionary authority exercised by the Agency under
the grant agreement. Under rule~3, [024] is now coded
\texttt{ISO\_COERCIVE} + \texttt{LOGIC\_STATE}: the grant agreement
is itself a legal instrument, and the suspension and termination
clauses are coercive in the regulative sense even where the wording
is administratively neutral. The refined rule~3 also implicates [026]
(the proactive roadmap-setting passage) for the same co-coding. The
Pass~1 notes are frozen and this consequence is not retroactively
applied to [026]; the change is logged as v2-006 in
\texttt{Codebook\_v2\_Inductive.md}~\S9 and is treated as in force for
Ch~4 and Ch~5 purposes.

\paragraph{Substantive finding.}
The descriptive backbone of Pattern~B is the gap between the
autonomy stated at [021] and the operative control exercised at [024]
and [026]. A hired technocrat, neither a peer scientist nor an
elected official, holds decisive authority over capital continuity
through legally neutral grant-mechanics language. Whether that gap is
best read as a deliberate institutional-design feature (state
oversight wrapped in the language of administrative discretion) or as
a constitutive contradiction (the autonomy promise and the
termination clause cannot both fully hold) is the interpretive
question deferred to \Cref{ch:discussion}.

\section{Pattern C --- State social-equity template}
\label{sec:res-pattern-c}

Pattern~C names a small set of near-identical passages embedding
gender balance, female-researcher empowerment, and
underrepresented-group mandates. The passages recur across
instruments and sections of the Work Programme with minimal
variation, which is the descriptive evidence that the wording is
templated rather than authored for each setting. The pattern operates
at the level of state-mandated social policy inserted into the
innovation instrument, distinct from the organisational normative
standard captured by \texttt{NORM\_DEI}.

\paragraph{The clearest template evidence.}
The near-identity of the team-composition passages at [047]
(Pathfinder Open) and [063] (Pathfinder Challenges) is the cleanest
template signature in the corpus. The wording covers gender balance,
female-researcher promotion, and underrepresented-group inclusion in
the same order and in close-to-identical phrasing across two
different instruments. Authored prose tracking instrument-specific
considerations would be expected to differ; the textual near-identity
is the evidence that the passage is a standardised insert rather than
an instrument-specific commitment.

\paragraph{The wider cluster and the tiebreaker.}
The same template surfaces at the Strategic Goals level at [001], the
institutional self-monitoring commitment at [017], the Challenge team
mandate at [112], and the STEP eligibility framing at [175]. The
tiebreaker architecture at [059] reinforces the pattern by ranking
gender balance above SME participation and above Member State
geographic representation. The tiebreaker is the strongest evidence
that gender mainstreaming is treated as a higher-priority state
commitment than the two priorities that more directly serve the
instrument's own stated success criteria (market participation and
geographic cohesion).

\paragraph{Rule~4 and the frozen-notes consequence.}
Pass~2 introduced coding rule~4, which disciplines the
\texttt{NORM\_DEI} / \texttt{LOGIC\_STATE} co-coding boundary.
State-mandated equity templates route through \texttt{LOGIC\_STATE};
\texttt{NORM\_DEI} is reserved for credible-plan organisational
standards with evaluation weight. Every Pattern~C instance in Pass~1
had already coded \texttt{LOGIC\_STATE} alone; rule~4 codifies that
practice. The frozen-notes consequence concerns [001]: its Pass~1
addendum self-flagged its \texttt{LOGIC\_TENSION} coding as a
constructed-argument tension, which rule~1 (surface-visibility) would
withdraw. The v1 notes are frozen and the change is not retroactively
applied. The change is logged as v2-011 in
\texttt{Codebook\_v2\_Inductive.md}~\S9; for Ch~4 and Ch~5 purposes
[001] is treated as \texttt{LOGIC\_STATE} only.

\paragraph{Standardised at the legislative layer.}
The textual near-identity across instruments is consistent with
insertion at the EU legislative layer rather than at the EIC's own
programmatic layer. The Horizon Europe Regulation imposes gender
mainstreaming obligations on the entire programme; the Pattern~C
passages read as the EIC's compliance reproduction of those
obligations rather than as a programmatic commitment authored at
EIC level. The interpretive question --- why state-equity priorities
are inserted into an innovation funding instrument, and why they are
ranked above the instrument's own market and geographic priorities in
the tiebreaker --- is deferred to \Cref{ch:discussion}.

\section{Pattern D --- Challenge-chapter logic succession template}
\label{sec:res-pattern-d}

Pattern~D names a chapter-level configuration: each Pathfinder
Challenge chapter reproduces a consistent logic succession across its
sub-sections, in the same order and with the same logic at each
position, across substantively different technology domains. The
pattern operates above the passage level. Underlying passage codings
are individually correct; Pattern~D is what becomes visible only when
the codings of a whole Challenge chapter are read together.

\paragraph{The template structure.}
Each Challenge chapter follows the same four-step succession. Scope
and Background sit in \texttt{LOGIC\_STATE}, framing the Challenge
through state-defined strategic relevance and sectoral framing.
Specific Objectives shift to \texttt{LOGIC\_SCIENCE}, articulating the
methodology and capability advancement the Challenge seeks. The
Portfolio Approach passes through \texttt{LOGIC\_PROFESSION},
formalising Programme Manager direction and portfolio composition.
Expected Outcomes and Impacts return to \texttt{LOGIC\_STATE},
articulating societal, environmental, and sovereignty goals. The
succession is not a smooth blending; each sub-section sits cleanly
in one dominant logic, and the transitions occur at sub-section
boundaries rather than within paragraphs.

\paragraph{Instances and confirmation across three domains.}
The template was first identified at Challenge II.2.1 (Advanced
Materials for Miniaturised Energy Harvesting Systems), in entries
[095]--[116]. It was then tested against Challenge II.2.2
(Biotechnology for Healthy Ageing), in the same entry range, and
against Challenge II.2.3 (DeepRAP), in entries [117]--[129]. The
three Challenges cover energy materials, biotechnology, and
artificial intelligence respectively. The substantive technology, the
disciplinary vocabulary, and the expected outputs differ
considerably between them. The logic configuration across
sub-sections does not.

\paragraph{Codebook-level result.}
Pattern~D is a pure analytical observation. No new code is added, no
flag-row recoding is required, and no coding rule is introduced. The
underlying passage codings (\texttt{LOGIC\_STATE},
\texttt{LOGIC\_SCIENCE}, \texttt{LOGIC\_PROFESSION},
\texttt{LOGIC\_STATE}) are individually correct in each Challenge.
Pattern~D is the configuration property of how those codings are
ordered at chapter scale, and there is no passage-level code that
can express an order across sub-sections by construction.

\paragraph{Connection to the pipeline succession.}
Pattern~D enriches the macro-level finding recorded at
\cref{sec:res-profile}. The Science~$\to$~Market~$\to$~State
succession across the innovation pipeline (from low-TRL Pathfinder
to high-TRL STEP) is reproduced as an intra-chapter succession
within every Challenge. The state's institutional reproduction
therefore operates at two scales simultaneously: across the
pipeline, and within each Challenge chapter independent of
technological content. The reading of that two-scale reproduction as
a meso-level isomorphism distinct from the passage-level
\texttt{ISO\_*} codes is the interpretive move deferred to
\Cref{ch:discussion}.

\section{Pattern E --- STEP circular market validation}
\label{sec:res-pattern-e}

Pattern~E names a structural relationship across the STEP eligibility
and award architecture: the state constructs the market signal it
then requires as proof of market legitimacy. The pattern is not
visible in any single passage. It emerges across the catalytic
mechanism, the market-interest eligibility criterion, the anchor
position, and the market-failure award criterion read as one
configuration. Pass~2 confirmed the pattern and codified its
coding-level consequence as rule~2.

\paragraph{The four entries that constitute the configuration.}
[132] sets out the catalytic-investment mechanism: STEP is designed
to attract private capital around an EIC anchor. [140] and [141]
establish \emph{demonstrated market interest} as an eligibility
criterion: applicants must show a non-legally-binding pre-commitment
from a qualified investor for at least 20\% of the target round.
[145] specifies the EIC's prospective participation as a 20--33\%
anchor in the same round. [176] adds, as an award criterion,
\emph{demonstrated market failure}: the requested amount cannot be
fully financed by private investors without EIC participation.

\paragraph{Why the configuration is structurally circular.}
The eligibility criterion (market interest) and the award criterion
(market failure) are only simultaneously satisfiable by using the
EIC's prospective anchor as the lever to obtain the private
pre-commitment. A qualified investor commits the 20\% pre-commitment
because the EIC will co-invest at 20--33\%; the EIC then accepts that
pre-commitment as evidence of market interest while separately
requiring evidence that the round cannot close without EIC
participation. The market signal the state requires is constructed by
the state's own prospective participation. At an anchor position of
20--33\% of the round, the private investors who supply the market
signal are not independently validating the company; they are
co-investing around a dominant state anchor.

\paragraph{Rule~2 and the codebook-level result.}
Pattern~E is recorded as a pure analytical observation across the
[132], [140], [141], [145], [176] configuration. It is not coded as
\texttt{LOGIC\_TENSION} (rule~1 reserves that code for
surface-visible conflict; the circularity is structural rather than
textually acknowledged). The codebook-level consequence is rule~2,
which disciplines \texttt{LOGIC\_STATE} and \texttt{LOGIC\_MARKET}
co-coding in STEP catalytic-investment passages: the primary coding
is \texttt{LOGIC\_STATE}; \texttt{LOGIC\_MARKET} co-codes only where
market mechanics are operationally surface-visible in the passage.
Rule~2 prevents Pattern~E's market-vocabulary surface from inflating
\texttt{LOGIC\_MARKET} codings across passages where the state is
the organising actor.

\paragraph{Two interpretive readings deferred to Ch~5.}
Two readings of the configuration are available and the data do not
decide between them. One reads the circularity as a fundamental
contradiction: the state claims to test market readiness while
eliminating the independent market assessment it claims to test for.
The other reads it as a deliberate developmental-state design: the
state constructs market conditions it then uses as legitimation,
consistent with mission-oriented investment and with Asian
state-anchored fund structures. The choice between the two is the
interpretive question taken up in \Cref{ch:discussion}.

\section{Pattern F --- TRL as cross-pillar legitimation artefact}
\label{sec:res-pattern-f}

Pattern~F names a property of the Technology Readiness Level (TRL)
framework as it operates inside the Work Programme. TRL functions
simultaneously across two of Scott's pillars within the same
passages, and Pass~2 identifies a third axis along which it carries
legitimation. The pattern is observable in single passages where the
two pillar codes co-occur; the wider mimetic axis becomes legible
once those passages are read against the DARPA finding recorded at
\cref{sec:res-step-mimesis}.

\paragraph{The two-pillar co-coding.}
As \texttt{REG\_TRL} (regulative pillar), TRL performs the gate
function: it sets the eligibility threshold for each instrument,
draws the scope boundary between Pathfinder and STEP, and routes
budget allocation along the pipeline. As \texttt{COG\_LINEAR}
(cognitive pillar), TRL is the taken-for-granted developmental
ladder, treated as a neutral scientific measurement of how mature a
technology is. The two codes are not in tension. The cognitive
register provides the scientific-measurement surface; the regulative
function performs the gatekeeping beneath. The configuration is
visible at [012] (TRL ranges defining the instruments), [044] (TRL
as a gatekeeping criterion in Pathfinder evaluation), and [046] (TRL
embedded in the Open call scope), and recurs across the Pathfinder
evaluation passages.

\paragraph{The codebook-level result.}
Pattern~F is recorded as a pure analytical observation. The v1
definitions of \texttt{REG\_TRL} and \texttt{COG\_LINEAR} are
unchanged. The v1 Coding Instructions already permit a single
passage to be coded both \texttt{REG\_TRL} and \texttt{COG\_LINEAR},
and Pass~2 introduces no rule disciplining that co-coding. The
finding is a relational property of how two correctly-applied codes
interact, not a coding ambiguity to resolve. Collapsing the two
codes into one would erase the very contrast between regulative gate
and cognitive ladder that makes the legitimation work analytically
readable.

\paragraph{The triple load.}
Pass~2 adds a third axis. The TRL framework was developed by NASA
and adopted across US-defence procurement before its uptake into EU
research and innovation policy; that provenance carries
mimetic-isomorphic legitimacy in DiMaggio and Powell's sense. The
artefact is therefore doing legitimation work along three axes
simultaneously: cognitively as a taken-for-granted measurement
ladder, regulatively as an eligibility gate, and mimetically through
its US-defence provenance. The mimetic axis is the cross-link to
\cref{sec:res-step-mimesis}: the same mimetic borrowing that
explicitly underwrites STEP's design (DARPA, Temasek, Chinese
Government Guidance Funds) is also embedded, less visibly, in the
TRL ladder that gates Pathfinder. Pattern~F is therefore the
analytically densest single site in the corpus. The cross-pillar
interpretation, which sets Scott, DiMaggio and Powell, and Suchman
on the same artefact, is the interpretive task taken up in
\Cref{ch:discussion}.

\section{STEP additional findings}
\label{sec:res-step-additional}

The STEP section carries three further findings that complement
Pattern~E without themselves forming patterns at the same level: the
state's absorption of market vocabulary as its own legitimation
register, the Sovereignty Seal as a portable legitimacy artefact,
and the explicit mimetic benchmarking against DARPA, Temasek, and
the Chinese Government Guidance Funds. The section closes with a
descriptive characterisation of STEP as a hybrid instrument that
deploys private-equity mechanics through state tender architecture.

\subsection{State absorbing market vocabulary as legitimation
register}
\label{sec:res-step-vocabulary}

The STEP section, anchored at [130] and developed across
[132]--[180], deploys a sustained register of market vocabulary
(\enquote{market gap}, \enquote{Venture Debt}, \enquote{equity},
\enquote{deal flow}). Read as a register, the vocabulary supplies
the legitimation for state intervention: it justifies the EIC's
presence by naming a market problem the market itself is presented
as unable to solve. Read as evidence of organising logic, the same
passages are governed by the state. The actor that defines the
\enquote{market gap}, that authorises the catalytic instrument, and
that issues the decisions is the Commission. \texttt{LOGIC\_STATE}
organises the passages; \texttt{LOGIC\_MARKET} supplies the
vocabulary.

\paragraph{Rule~7 and the threshold for primary coding.}
Pass~2 codifies this distinction as coding rule~7 (the
vocabulary-vs-organising standard): a logic is coded only where it
organises the passage. Vocabulary-level signals in procedural,
administrative, or budgetary passages do not in themselves warrant
primary coding. Rule~7 generalises two precedents that were already
informally observed in Pass~1: the [012] precedent, in which
state-as-administrative-funder language sat below the
\texttt{LOGIC\_STATE} threshold because the surrounding passage was
budgetary rather than state-organising, and the [014] precedent, in
which venture-capital vocabulary in budgetary sentences sat below the
\texttt{LOGIC\_MARKET} threshold for the same reason. Rule~7 keeps
the coding scheme honest about which logic governs and which logic
provides surface vocabulary.

\paragraph{Where \texttt{LOGIC\_MARKET} genuinely organises.}
The exceptions to the register reading are concentrated in Table~9 of
the STEP section. The market-opportunity criterion at [172] and the
business-model criterion at [173] place market logic in the
evaluative frame: the criterion is whether the company can grow as a
market actor, judged in market terms. The cost-and-performance
dimension of the innovation criterion at [169] does the same at the
product level. These three passages are the clearest sites in the
corpus where market logic actually organises the evaluative frame
rather than supplying its vocabulary.

\subsection{The Sovereignty Seal as field-bridging legitimacy
artefact}
\label{sec:res-step-seal}

The Sovereignty (STEP) Seal is a state-issued credential that travels
across institutional fields. Once awarded, it serves as a signal that
the project has been judged by the EIC to meet the STEP eligibility
and strategic-value criteria, and that signal can be used in
settings beyond the STEP award itself.

\paragraph{The mechanism and its reach.}
The Seal unlocks access to InvestEU Venture Debt ([152], [161]) and
enables access to Business Acceleration Services. Its reach is
inter-institutional: it carries weight in public EU programmes that
the EIC does not itself administer, and the STEP text signals an
expectation that it will carry weight with private investors as a
proxy for state validation of strategic relevance.

\paragraph{The NO~GO award path.}
The most analytically revealing property of the Seal is that it can
be awarded even on a NO~GO investment decision where merit criteria
are met but budget is unavailable ([166]). The Seal is decoupled
from the investment decision itself. The state certifies strategic
value independently of whether it commits capital to the certified
project. Stripped of the capital commitment, the Seal stands as a
legitimacy instrument in its own right: a credential issued by the
state and designed to travel.

\paragraph{Substantive finding.}
The combination of inter-institutional reach and decoupling from the
investment decision is the descriptive backbone of the Seal as a
field-bridging artefact. The Suchman reading of the Seal as a
designed instrument carrying both pragmatic legitimacy (it gives
access to resources) and moral legitimacy (the state certifies
strategic value) is the interpretive move taken up in
\Cref{ch:discussion}.

\subsection{Mimetic isomorphism against DARPA, Temasek and Chinese
GGFs}
\label{sec:res-step-mimesis}

The Work Programme explicitly benchmarks STEP against US and Asian
state-investment models. The cited reference points are DARPA on the
US side and Temasek and the Chinese Government Guidance Funds on the
Asian side. The citation is a direct invocation of those models as
the comparators against which STEP is designed.

\paragraph{Selective adoption.}
The adoption is selective rather than wholesale. STEP reproduces the
equity and catalytic-investment mechanics that characterise the
cited models, but it embeds those mechanics in EU procurement
architecture: the Funding and Tenders Portal, quarterly evaluation
batches, the Commission Award Decision, the jury process, and the
50-page business plan with Freedom-to-Operate analysis. The result
is a hybrid that uses the cited models for its investment mechanics
while preserving the EU's own administrative architecture. STEP does
not fully replicate the models it cites; it borrows from them.

\paragraph{Cross-link to Pattern~F.}
The mimetic axis is not confined to the instrument-design level of
STEP. As recorded at \cref{sec:res-pattern-f}, the TRL framework
that gates Pathfinder also carries US-defence provenance. Mimetic
borrowing therefore operates across both instruments: TRL on the
Pathfinder side, equity-anchor design on the STEP side. The
substantive observation for the chapter is that the EIC as a whole
draws on US innovation finance and US-defence procurement as
reference models at two different points in the pipeline.

\section{Sustainability as an adaptive state-policy entry vector}
\label{sec:res-sustainability}

Pass~1 raised the hypothesis, at [096], that sustainability framing
might recur across Challenge chapters as a standardised state-logic
insert, analogous to Pattern~C (the State Social Equity Template).
Pass~2 tested the universality of this candidate template and did not
confirm it. The decisive negative case is Challenge II.2.2
(Biotechnology for Healthy Ageing), where no sustainability or
environmental language is present. Sustainability is therefore
subject-matter-dependent, not a universal Challenge insert. The
candidate pattern is recorded as not confirmed.

\paragraph{The four-mechanism inventory.}
The substantive finding from the universality test is preserved as a
four-mechanism inventory. When sustainability does appear in the
corpus, it enters via one of four distinct mechanisms.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Commission Communication policy-citation} ([099]).
    The Challenge cites a Commission Communication that itself
    carries the sustainability frame, and the frame flows through by
    institutional inheritance. The Communication does the
    sustainability work; the Challenge receives it.
  \item \textbf{Explicit environmental-consequence framing} ([096]).
    Sustainability appears as descriptive context establishing the
    problem the Challenge addresses, for example IoT energy
    consumption or battery waste. Here the environmental frame is
    authored into the Challenge text directly.
  \item \textbf{Sector-definition} (around [117]--[120]).
    Sustainability is constitutive of the technology field itself,
    visible in STEP's treatment of clean and resource-efficient
    technologies as a critical-area category. The frame is carried
    by how the sector is named rather than by an added
    sustainability clause.
  \item \textbf{Technical efficiency-proxy} ([125], [129]).
    Sustainability is absorbed into methodology requirements via
    constrained-resource specifications, for example the
    compute-constraint requirements in DeepRAP, without explicit
    environmental framing or policy citation. The environmental
    work is done implicitly through resource bounds on the
    technical specification.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Analytical contrast with Pattern~C.}
The four mechanisms set up a contrast with Pattern~C that sharpens
both findings. Pattern~C operates as a universal insert reproduced
in near-identical wording across instruments and Challenges
regardless of subject matter. Sustainability operates as an adaptive
entry vector that adjusts its expression to the content of each
Challenge. The institutional architecture therefore carries a small
set of state priorities universally (gender mainstreaming) while
letting other state priorities (sustainability) enter through
subject-matter-appropriate channels. The reading of this contrast as
a meso-level finding about how the state distributes its priorities
across the institutional architecture is the interpretive move
deferred to \Cref{ch:discussion}.

\section{Chapter summary}
\label{sec:res-summary}

The chapter has described the empirical findings of the readthrough
in the order in which the synthesis builds them. The logic profile
across the corpus, set out in \cref{sec:res-profile}, distributes the
four institutional logics unevenly across Pathfinder and STEP and
records a Science~$\to$~Market~$\to$~State succession across the
innovation pipeline, reproduced as an intra-chapter succession in
every Challenge.

The six patterns confirmed in Pass~2 follow that profile. Pattern~A
(\cref{sec:res-pattern-a}) names the textual move that presents
conflicting logics as naturally complementary. Pattern~B
(\cref{sec:res-pattern-b}) names the Programme Manager authority
cluster and its intensity gradient from stated autonomy to
contractual termination authority. Pattern~C
(\cref{sec:res-pattern-c}) names the standardised social-equity
template inserted into the instrument at the EU legislative layer.
Pattern~D (\cref{sec:res-pattern-d}) names the chapter-level logic
succession that recurs across substantively different Challenge
domains. Pattern~E (\cref{sec:res-pattern-e}) names the structural
circularity in STEP, in which the state constructs the market signal
it then requires. Pattern~F (\cref{sec:res-pattern-f}) names TRL's
cross-pillar legitimation work, including the triple load along
cognitive, regulative, and mimetic axes.

The STEP additional findings (\cref{sec:res-step-additional}) record
three further results that complement Pattern~E without forming
patterns at the same level: the state's absorption of market
vocabulary as a legitimation register, the Sovereignty Seal as a
field-bridging legitimacy artefact, and the explicit mimetic
benchmarking against DARPA, Temasek, and the Chinese Government
Guidance Funds. The hybrid-instrument table summarises how market
mechanics and state access architecture sit side by side without
integration. The sustainability inventory
(\cref{sec:res-sustainability}) records the four-mechanism finding
preserved from the negative-universality test of the candidate
sustainability template.

The chapter has held to a descriptive register throughout. The
interpretive readings of these findings --- Pattern~E as a
developmental-state design rather than a contradiction, Pattern~F's
triple load across Scott's pillars, Pattern~C's ranking of
social-equity priorities above the instrument's market and
geographic priorities, and Pattern~D as a meso-level isomorphism
distinct from the passage-level \texttt{ISO\_*} mechanisms --- are
taken up in \Cref{ch:discussion}.
