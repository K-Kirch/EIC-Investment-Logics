### 0.4 Coding rules in force (v2 §9)

1. **Surface-visibility for `LOGIC_TENSION`** — tension must be legible in the text.
2. **STEP catalytic co-coding** — `LOGIC_MARKET` co-codes with `LOGIC_STATE` only where market mechanics are operationally surface-visible.
3. **Contractual discretionary coercion** — `ISO_COERCIVE` extended to grant-agreement-based termination, suspension, and mandated amendment.
4. **`NORM_DEI` / `LOGIC_STATE` scope** — state-mandated equity templates route through `LOGIC_STATE`; `NORM_DEI` reserved for credible-plan organisational standards.
5. **BAS coding rule** — `LOGIC_PROFESSION` organises BAS provision where professional-services framing is present; `LOGIC_MARKET` co-codes as actor pool.
6. **Structural state inference** — `LOGIC_STATE` requires the state to organise the passage as actor; external-inference observations are noted, not coded.
7. **Vocabulary-vs-organising standard** — VC, administrative-funder, management-register, and normative-identity vocabulary do not in themselves warrant primary logic coding in procedural / administrative passages.

### 0.5 Post-hoc correction (Batch 65, thesis-writing pass)

The §2.5 mimetic-isomorphism finding was reformulated during thesis writing after a corpus review confirmed that the Work Programme does not name DARPA, Temasek, or the Chinese Government Guidance Funds. The corpus benchmarks EIC performance generically against "the USA and Asia" at Strategic Goal 4; specific institutional comparators are supplied by the developmental-state literature.

## 2. Key Analytical Findings (v2)

### 2.1 Logic Succession Across the Innovation Pipeline

Unchanged in substance from Pass 1. As TRL increases, the dominant institutional logic shifts from `LOGIC_SCIENCE` to `LOGIC_MARKET` to `LOGIC_STATE`.

- **Pathfinder (TRL 1–4):** `LOGIC_SCIENCE` dominates the Specific Objectives of all three Challenge chapters.
- **Pathfinder Challenges — Expected Outcomes/Impacts:** `LOGIC_STATE` increasingly organises as outputs move toward societal and sovereignty goals.
- **STEP Scale Up:** `LOGIC_STATE` dominates the instrument architecture; `LOGIC_MARKET` is genuinely operative only in Table 9 award criteria, mediated by state selection elsewhere.

`[v2] addition (Pattern D):` The Science → Market → State succession across TRL is reproduced as an *intra-chapter* succession within every Challenge (`LOGIC_STATE` scope → `LOGIC_SCIENCE` objectives → `LOGIC_PROFESSION` portfolio → `LOGIC_STATE` impacts). The succession is therefore institutionalised at two scales simultaneously: across the innovation pipeline and within each Challenge chapter. The meso-level reproduction is itself a finding — see §2.12.

### 2.2 Pattern A — Tension-Dissolution as Institutional Practice

**Formalised in Pass 2.** Logics are presented as naturally complementary, dissolving tension that is structurally present. Where Pass 1 sometimes coded this as `LOGIC_TENSION`, rule 1 (surface-visibility) now reserves `LOGIC_TENSION` for textually legible conflict and routes constructed-argument tensions into Pattern A as an analytical observation.

**Confirmed instances:** [005], [006]/[007], [010], [019], [039], [056], [057], [061], [082], [102]. **Pass 2 extensions:** [003], [008] (Overview / Strategic Goals); [044] (cross-bullet gatekeeper tension); [109] ([108]→[109] cross-entry).

**Key analytical move:** Pattern A is a legitimacy strategy in its own right — presenting logics as complementary *performs* institutional coherence rather than arguing for it. The dissolved conflicts (open science vs. IP exploitation; scientific curiosity vs. market-readiness; "any field" vs. "named technology domains") are real conflicts in practice; the text's silence about them is the finding.

**Most consequential instance:** [082] — state-defined Challenge relevance embedded as the primary sub-criterion of scientific excellence. The text shapes how applicants must frame their science to receive funding while presenting the framing as a science quality criterion.

### 2.3 Pattern F — TRL as Cross-Pillar Legitimation Artefact

**Formalised in Pass 2** (replaces Pass 1 §2.3 "TRL as Institutional Disguise"). TRL operates simultaneously across two of Scott's pillars within the same passages:

- `REG_TRL` (regulative pillar): the gate function — eligibility threshold, scope boundary, budget allocation, instrument routing.
- `COG_LINEAR` (cognitive pillar): the taken-for-granted developmental ladder, borrowing measurement legitimacy from its NASA / US-defence origin.

The two codes are **not in tension**; they co-code in the same passages. The cognitive pillar provides scientific-register surface cover; the regulative pillar performs the gatekeeping beneath. The disguise depoliticises what are political decisions about where in the innovation pipeline public money flows.

`[v2] triple-load finding (new to Pass 2):` The TRL artefact is doing legitimation work along **three** axes simultaneously:

1. **Cognitive** — scientific-measurement register (Suchman: cognitive legitimacy via taken-for-grantedness).
2. **Regulative** — eligibility gate (Scott's regulative pillar).
3. **Mimetic** — US-defence provenance, NASA origin (DiMaggio & Powell's mimetic isomorphism; cross-references §2.5).

This triple-load is what makes the artefact analytically dense. Pattern F cannot be reduced to any single pillar / mechanism — it is the *co-operation* of three legitimation axes within one artefact.

**Cited entries:** [012], [044], [046]; recurs across Pathfinder evaluation passages.

### 2.5 Mimetic Isomorphism: STEP against the developmental-state comparator set (analyst-supplied)

Reformulated during thesis-writing review (see §0.5 below); substance retained, framing corrected:

STEP is legible as mimetic isomorphism at the instrument-design level when read against the developmental-state comparator set supplied by the secondary literature (Mazzucato 2018; Li 2025; Luong 2021): DARPA on the US side, Temasek and the Chinese Government Guidance Funds on the Asian side. The corpus itself benchmarks EIC performance against "the USA and Asia" at Strategic Goal 4 without naming any specific institutional referent. Structural mimicry is selective — the equity / catalytic-investment mechanics are reproduced, embedded in EU procurement architecture (Funding and Tenders Portal, quarterly batches, Commission Award Decision) — and the result is a hybrid that does not fully replicate the comparator templates. The reading identifies the comparators' traces in the corpus through structural resemblance rather than through explicit citation, consistent with DiMaggio & Powell's original account of mimetic isomorphism operating without required textual acknowledgement.

`[v2] cross-link to Pattern F:` The TRL framework itself carries DARPA / US-defence provenance. Pattern F's third axis (mimetic isomorphism) is therefore not isolated to STEP — mimetic borrowing operates across Pathfinder (TRL ladder) and STEP (equity instrument) alike. The institutional logic of the EIC as a whole is partly *constituted* by mimetic borrowing from US innovation finance and US defence procurement.

### 2.7 Pattern E — STEP Structural Mandate for Circular Market Validation

**Formalised in Pass 2** with codified co-coding rule (rule 2).

STEP simultaneously requires:

1. **Demonstrated market interest** — pre-commitment from a qualified investor (20% of target round, [141])
2. **Demonstrated market failure** — amount cannot be fully financed by private investors without EIC ([176])

These two are only simultaneously satisfiable by using EIC's prospective 20–33% anchor participation ([145]) as the lever to obtain the private pre-commitment. The private investor commits *because* EIC will co-invest; the EIC requires the commitment as proof of market interest. The market signal the state requires is constructed by the state's own potential participation.

Furthermore, the pre-commitment is explicitly non-legally binding ([141], footnote 177), and EIC holds 20–33% of the total round. At this scale, private investors are not independently validating the company; they are co-investing around a dominant state anchor.

`[v2] codebook-level result:` The Pass 1 open question — whether the catalytic state-investor position warrants a new code or a refinement of `LOGIC_STATE` — is **resolved in the negative**. Pattern E is a *relational/structural* finding across a configuration of passages; codes apply at the unit-of-analysis level (sentence/clause). Rule 2 disciplines the co-coding of `LOGIC_STATE` + `LOGIC_MARKET` in catalytic-investment contexts: `LOGIC_MARKET` co-codes only where market mechanics are surface-visible in the passage. The structural circularity is recorded as Pattern E (analytical observation), not as a `LOGIC_TENSION` coding.

**Theoretical question deferred to Ch 5:** Whether this represents a fundamental contradiction (state claims to test market readiness while eliminating independent market assessment) or a deliberate developmental-state design (state constructs market conditions it then deploys as legitimation). The latter aligns with Mazzucato's mission-oriented investment model and Chinese Government Guidance Fund structures rather than liberal market VC logic.

### 2.10 Sustainability — Candidate Pattern Tested and Not Confirmed

**Hypothesis (Pass 1, [096]):** Sustainability framing recurs across Challenge chapters as a standardised state legitimation insert, analogous to Pattern C.

**Result (Pass 2):** **Not confirmed.** The universality test fails at II.2.2 (Biotechnology for Healthy Ageing): no sustainability or environmental language present. Sustainability is **subject-matter-dependent**, not a universal template.

**Substantive finding preserved (four mechanisms when sustainability does appear):**

1. **Commission Communication policy-citation** ([099]) — sustainability flows through institutional inheritance from a cited Commission Communication.
2. **Explicit environmental-consequence framing** ([096]) — sustainability appears as descriptive context establishing the problem.
3. **Sector-definition** ([117]–[120]) — sustainability is constitutive of the technology field itself (clean / resource-efficient technologies as a STEP critical-area category).
4. **Technical efficiency-proxy** ([125]/[129]) — sustainability absorbed into methodology requirements via constrained-resource specifications, no explicit environmental framing or policy citation.

`[v2] analytical value as contrast with Pattern C:` Pattern C operates as a *universal* insert reproduced in near-identical wording regardless of subject matter. Sustainability operates as an *adaptive* state-policy entry vector. The contrast sharpens what Pattern C actually templates: the institutional architecture carries a small set of state priorities universally (gender mainstreaming) while letting other state priorities (sustainability) enter through subject-matter-appropriate channels. This is a meso-level finding about how the state distributes its priorities across the institutional architecture — universal templating reserved for cross-cutting normative commitments; adaptive templating for substantive policy content.

Pass 1 §2.10 framed three mechanisms; Pass 2 identifies four (sector-definition added) and reframes the finding as a *negative* result on the universality hypothesis, with the four-mechanism inventory preserved as analytical contrast.

## 5. Theoretical Implications (Pass 2 refresh)

### 5.1 For Scott's three pillars

Pattern F (§2.3) is the most significant Pass 2 refinement at the pillar level. The EIC instruments operate primarily through the regulative pillar (legal mandates, eligibility rules, coercive authority — now including contractual discretionary coercion per rule 3) with normative pillar elements (professional standards, FAIR principles, KYC/AML norms, DEI templates per Pattern C) and a more substantial cognitive pillar presence than Pass 1 acknowledged.

`[v2] Pattern F finding:` The cognitive pillar does not stand alone — the TRL artefact shows the cognitive pillar functioning as **surface cover for the regulative pillar within the same passages**. The three pillars are not analytically separable in every instance; they co-operate in some artefacts. Pattern F is the clearest instance and warrants Ch 5 treatment as a cross-pillar legitimation mechanism.

### 5.2 For DiMaggio & Powell isomorphism

- **Mimetic isomorphism** — analyst-supplied reading against the developmental-state comparator set (DARPA, Temasek, Chinese GGFs) drawn from the secondary literature; the corpus benchmarks generically against "the USA and Asia" at Strategic Goal 4 without naming specific institutions.
